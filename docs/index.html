What works in this public version?

Image browsing and zoom, reference-label review, notes, evidence selection, local image import, appearance-based similarity, and JSON or illustrated HTML report export all run in your browser. Sample reviews and findings are saved in this browser when storage is available. Imported images stay in the current tab.

Optional MobileNet CPU inference runs locally after you enable it. Arbitrary text-to-image search, retraining, shared accounts, and server storage are not connected. Dataset reference labels are not predictions. The benchmark page contains separate archived model evaluation results.

About the project

A workspace for understanding tissue images.

Tissology brings images, AI suggestions, and a researcher's observations into one place.

Tissology is a research prototype for exploring microscope images of tissue. Open real images, compare AI suggestions with published labels, and record your observations with the supporting images attached.

It connects the steps from inspecting an image to sharing a research finding. You can try the public collection without an account or a pathology background.

From the public collection

Real images from the public sample, with their published dataset labels.

What am I looking at?

The pink and purple pictures are small pieces of microscope images, called tiles. This collection contains 36 tiles from a public colorectal tissue dataset. They span nine categories, including fat tissue, muscle, and lymphocytes—a type of immune cell.

Each sample comes with a reference label: the category assigned in the original dataset. If you enable local analysis, the model produces its own prediction. Your review records whether you agree, would choose another label, or are unsure. These are kept separate so you can inspect disagreements.

What can I do here?

  1. Explore a collection

    Browse the prepared tiles, filter by tissue category, and zoom in. You can also bring in images from your own device.

  2. Compare and question

    Find images with similar colors and textures, or optionally run a tissue classifier on your computer. Inspect its suggestions alongside the image.

  3. Record your interpretation

    Review labels, add notes, and select images as evidence. A finding is your written observation together with those supporting images.

  4. Take your work with you

    Download an illustrated report with the images embedded, or export the structured reviews and predictions for later use.

A public window into the research project

The full Tissology project includes collection analysis, image search, researcher review, and evidence-backed reports. This public version makes a selection of those workflows available without an account or a connection to the research server.

The optional browser classifier is a lightweight MobileNet model. The Benchmark page shows separate, archived results from the project's CONCH classifier. They are different models: the benchmark numbers do not describe the predictions you run in this demo.

This is an exploratory research tool, not a diagnostic service. AI suggestions can be wrong, and the demo does not establish clinical reliability. It does not provide live CONCH analysis, model retraining, or shared team accounts.

Your images and your computer

The site serves files through GitHub Pages. Image inspection, similarity comparison, and optional AI analysis happen on your device. Imported images are not uploaded to the project owner's server.

Sample reviews and saved findings can stay in this browser. Imported images and standalone predictions last only for the current tab, so export work you want to keep. The user guide explains these storage differences and walks through each feature.

Getting started

Using Tissology

Explore tissue images, record your review, and turn selected evidence into a finding. Everything runs in your browser; no account is needed.

Your first finding, in four steps

  1. Open a collection. Choose Images to browse the 36 prepared tissue tiles.
  2. Inspect a tile. Click its image, use the zoom slider, and choose an assessment under Your review. Add a note and select Save review.
  3. Choose evidence. Select Select as evidence in the image dialog, or tick the checkboxes on gallery tiles.
  4. Write and export. Choose Write a finding →, enter a title and interpretation, then Save finding. In the saved finding, choose Download illustrated report for an HTML file with embedded images.
Browse, inspect, and review images

Use Find images to search filenames or tissue names. Tissue class filters the published reference labels; Review status filters your assessments. These filters do not filter model predictions. Use Clear filters to restore the collection.

Click a tile to inspect it. The zoom slider enlarges the image; scroll within it to see other areas. Previous and Next follow the current filtered order. Left and right arrow keys work when you are not typing in a field; Escape closes the dialog.

  • Confirm reference label: record agreement with the dataset label.
  • Assign a different label: choose your own tissue label.
  • Unsure: record uncertainty without choosing a replacement label.

Save review saves your assessment and note. Edits also save when you close the image or move to another image. A review does not retrain the model or replace the dataset reference label.

Run predictions on your own CPU
  1. Open Images and choose Enable local analysis · 15.1 MB. On first use, your browser downloads the model and its runtime. Wait for the Ready message.
  2. Choose whether to analyze the shown images or your selected evidence.
  3. Choose Run next batch · up to 20. Run it again to continue with unprocessed images. Completed images are skipped.
  4. Open an analyzed image to see its top three model scores. Use Export predictions JSON to keep the complete scores and model information.

Stop cancels downloading or computation and keeps completed predictions. To continue, enable analysis again. Unload model frees the model from memory. Remove saved model removes its explicit browser cache; normal browser caching may still keep runtime files.

For imported images, confirm that they are colorectal H&E tissue tiles before running the model. Analysis accepts square images at least 224 × 224 pixels and resizes them to 224 × 224. It does not accept whole slides or rectangular images for inference.

Inference uses your device's CPU. Images are not uploaded. The model is TIA MobileNetV3-Small, not CONCH. Its scores are not calibrated clinical confidence. It agreed with 23 of the 36 demo reference labels; this small sample is not an independent accuracy estimate.

Add your own images

Choose Add local images and select PNG, JPEG, or WebP files. Each selection can contain up to 100 images totaling 50 MB. Use the Collection menu to switch between your local images and the public sample.

Files stay on your device and are available only in this tab. You can inspect, annotate, compare, and attach them to findings. There is no supplied reference label for an imported image, so Confirm reference label is unavailable; use Assign a different label to record your own label.

Export local reviews and illustrated reports before reloading or closing the tab. Imported images and findings containing them are not restored on your next visit.

Find visually similar images

Open a tile and choose Find similar. The collection is ranked by color and texture resemblance to that tile, with the query image first. The existing filters are cleared when the search starts; you can apply filters afterward.

This comparison runs locally and does not need the CPU classifier download. It is not text search, CONCH semantic search, or evidence that two images show the same tissue. Use Clear ranking to return to the collection order.

Save findings and choose an export

Use tile checkboxes, Select shown images, or Select as evidence to attach images. Choose Write a finding →, enter a title and interpretation, and save. At least one evidence image is required.

A saved finding captures your reviews and any local predictions available for those images at that moment. Later reviews or predictions do not update that snapshot; save a new finding to capture the changes.

Understand labels and the benchmark

Reference label is the published dataset label. Local model prediction is generated by MobileNet on your CPU. Your review is your own assessment. The app keeps these separate.

Benchmark ↗ opens an archived CONCH evaluation with class metrics, a confusion matrix, and aggregate error analysis. Its results do not describe the browser MobileNet model. Benchmark findings are separate from image findings and last only for that benchmark tab; export them before leaving.

Tissology is a research prototype, not a clinical diagnostic system. The public version does not include retraining, shared accounts, or whole-slide analysis.

Troubleshooting
  • No images appear: clear the filters and check the selected collection. Local imports disappear after reload.
  • The analysis button is disabled: enable local analysis and wait for Ready. No computation begins until you run a batch.
  • No new images to analyze: the current set has already been processed, or your evidence selection is empty. Change the filters or selection.
  • An imported image is rejected: use a supported file type; for inference, use square colorectal H&E tiles at least 224 × 224 pixels and confirm the image type.
  • Download or model loading fails: check your connection, use a recent browser on the hosted HTTPS site, and retry. If needed, remove the saved model and enable analysis again.
  • The device is struggling: select fewer images, use Stop, and unload the model when finished.
  • Similarity or report export fails when opening local HTML files: use the hosted site; browser file restrictions can prevent reading images for these operations.
