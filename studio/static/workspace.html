How it works

Start with a collection of tissue images. Review predictions, find related images, and save findings with their evidence.

First time here?

Add a collection of supported image tiles to get started. A prepared example appears below when one is available.

  1. Open an image to inspect its prediction.
  2. Choose Find similar to explore related images.
  3. Select evidence and write a finding.
Try the 18-image collection →

Checking scan availability…

  1. Bring your images

    Choose Review images to upload images or select a folder. JPEG, PNG, and single-page TIFF tiles are supported. Whole slides need to be cut into tiles first.

    The current scanner classifies colorectal H&E tiles. Other image types can be imported for browsing.

  2. Inspect what the scan found

    A completed scan shows predicted tissue classes and image counts across the collection. Filter the results, then open an image to see its prediction and alternative scores.

    Choose Confirm, Correct, or Unsure to record your judgment. These reviews stay with the image; a correction does not automatically retrain the model.

  3. Find tissue using words or examples

    Open an image in a scanned collection and choose Find similar, or open Search images and try a prepared search. You can begin with an image without knowing its tissue name.

    Choose Select region to search using a smaller part of an image. Matches are other whole images; the search does not mark matching locations within them.

    Inspect the suggested matches and mark relevant or irrelevant examples to refine the search. Appearance descriptions are exploratory; search feedback changes the ranking within that collection.

  4. Turn selected images into a finding

    Use Write a finding to write your interpretation and attach supporting images from the same collection. Save the finding when you are ready to keep a version.

    Download its standalone report with embedded image evidence. Return to Saved work on the homepage to reopen a collection or study; saved findings are also listed inside their collection.

How reliable is the scan on my images?

Choose Check this scan inside a scanned collection. Set a review budget before starting, then label a fixed random sample with the predictions hidden. You can pause and continue the same check.

After every sampled image has a reference label, the result shows estimated accuracy and an uncertainty interval for the eligible, previously unreviewed images. You can check the current scan without training a new model. An incomplete check has no accuracy result; choose unsure when you cannot label an image.

Can I teach it from my corrections?

Yes. Open Teach this collection inside a scanned collection. Explicitly approve using your confirmed or corrected labels to train a candidate classifier.

Review a separate random sample with both predictions hidden, then inspect how the candidate performed. You decide whether to apply it. Improvement is checked, not assumed; original predictions and older findings retain their versions.

What does the example tissue study measure?

Example tissue study opens a separate workflow using 32 prepared breast regions. Explore tissue-area measurements, a fixed review sample, and corrected estimates with uncertainty intervals. You can also create another run on the prepared collection.

Existing reference annotations simulate the review in this example. Uploaded image tiles do not automatically become an area study.

How should I read the accuracy number?

The homepage reports classification accuracy on the named colorectal benchmark. It does not establish accuracy on your uploaded images, the quality of search results, or the accuracy of breast tissue-area measurements.

A scan is a preliminary way to organize images for inspection. Collection scans assign one tissue class per image; image counts are not tissue areas or cell counts.

What gets saved?

Imported collections, saved reviews, named findings, and studies are stored on this server. An unfinished description is a draft in your browser until you save it.

Download a report when you want a copy you can keep or share outside the workspace. The report includes the saved finding and its image evidence.
